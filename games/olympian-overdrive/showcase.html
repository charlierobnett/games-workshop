How we build a video game together — humans + AI

We're building a real video game — with an AI partner.

The AI (we call it Hobbes) can build incredibly fast. But here's the secret it can't do: it cannot tell what's actually FUN.

That's the human job. That's your job. A computer can make a game that works — but only a person can make a game that feels great.

The three pieces

🤖 Hobbes — the AI partner. It researches great games, writes the exact "rules" of our game (the spec), generates the code, and fixes its own bugs.

🎮 Olympian Overdrive — our game. A sports adventure where you climb a career (JV → Varsity → … → Olympics), and losing makes you stronger instead of just ending the run.

🔁 The Way We Work — a loop where humans bring taste & feel and AI brings speed & structure. Neither one builds a great game alone.

The deal

⚙️ AI is great at

  • Writing tons of code, fast
  • Following exact rules
  • Researching & organizing
  • Never getting tired

🧠 Humans are great at

  • Knowing what's fun
  • Feeling when something's "off"
  • Taste, style, surprise
  • Knowing when to break a rule

How a great game actually gets made

We don't just "make a game." We run a loop, and every trip around makes the game better. Here's one lap:

1Research

Study great games & ask deep questions. (This is where YOU come in.)

2Synthesize

The AI digests all the notes into clear, usable lessons.

3Spec

We write the exact rules of the game — every number, every feel.

4Build

The AI generates the code from the spec.

5Test (you!)

You play it and say what's fun, what's broken, what's missing.

6Refine

Back to the top — smarter every time.

Fun fact: right now we're even using this loop to research the best way for you to take notes. We take your findings that seriously.

Our weekly rhythm

📅 During the week — you play retro games and log what you find.

🛋️ Each weekend — we regroup, the AI synthesizes your notes, we update the spec, and build a new version.

🔁 Next week — you test the new build and keep researching. The game gets better every single week.

Your testing isn't a chore at the end — it's the steering wheel. Every lap is pointed by what you found fun.

🔎 Your mission: become Retro Game Detectives

Play the classics — NESSNES GenesisGame Boy — and figure out why the great ones feel great, and why the annoying parts annoy. Every game you test makes our game smarter.

What to pay attention to while you play

🎬 Menu & Start
Easy to know what to do? Did it look cool?
🕹️ Navigation & Controls
Did moving feel smooth, or clunky?
🏠 Home World / Hub
Was the "home base" fun to be in?
📈 Progression
Did getting stronger / unlocking stuff feel rewarding?
⚙️ Mechanics
What's the main thing you DO? Is it satisfying?
🗺️ Level Design
Were the levels interesting, or boring/confusing?
⭐ Power-ups / Items
Did they feel exciting and game-changing?
💥 Game Feel / Juice
Hits, sounds, screen shake — did it feel punchy?
⚖️ Fairness
When you lost, was it fair? Did you want to retry?

How you log it — catch the moments

You write two kinds of notes: Moments (something that hits you while you're playing — a great power-up, a cheap death, a boring stretch — logged right then) and a Wrap (a quick summary at the end of each session). The golden rule: catch it in the moment — don't wait until the end and try to remember, because you'll only remember the last thing.

In your end-of-session Wrap, the two big answers

✅ The ONE thing I'd STEAL

The single best idea worth putting in our game.

❌ The ONE thing that RUINED it

The single most annoying thing we should never do.
